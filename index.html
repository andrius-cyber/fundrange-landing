Funding is one of the hardest parts of running a lending business

Running a non-bank lender is demanding. You need to grow originations, manage portfolio quality, control defaults, meet regulatory requirements, improve operations — and at the same time make sure there is enough funding available at the right price, amount, and structure.

Funding is not only about finding someone willing to provide capital. It is about finding the right capital partner for your stage, product, geography, portfolio quality, and growth plans.

A young fintech, a fast-growing lender, and a mature originator will each need a different approach. They will also attract different types of investors.

FundRange helps you understand where you stand, which investors are realistic for you, and how to approach them in the right way.

Typical situations where we can help:

  • ✓Seeking a first institutional debt or equity funding partner
  • ✓Growth outpacing the current funding line
  • ✓Overpaying for funding or overly reliant on a single provider
  • ✓Considering forward flow, portfolio pledge, or securitisation structures
  • ✓Unsure which funding structure best fits the current stage and portfolio

If funding is becoming a constraint, FundRange can help.

Funding Structures We Support

We help with different types of debt and equity funding structures, and help lenders understand the practical differences between them.

Forward flow agreements Portfolio pledge facilities Securitisation-style structures Senior debt facilities Junior debt / first-loss capital Whole loan sales Portfolio acquisitions Portfolio refinancing Growth debt Equity introductions Hybrid debt/equity structures

A lower-priced facility may not always be the best solution if it comes with restrictive eligibility criteria, slow drawdowns, or limited flexibility. In many cases, flexibility can be more valuable than a slightly lower headline rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you charge lenders upfront? +
FundRange usually works on a success-based model. If no acceptable funding commitment is secured, no success fee is payable. In some cases, a separate advisory or preparation fee may be agreed if the company needs substantial work before it is ready for investors.
Do investors pay anything? +
No. The relationship is free of charge for investors. FundRange is paid by lenders when a successful funding transaction is completed.
What size of funding do you usually work with? +
We usually work with lenders looking to raise at least EUR 2 million in debt or equity funding. Larger transactions are especially relevant for institutional investors, but smaller cases may also be considered if the company is growing quickly or has a strong portfolio.
Which countries do you cover? +
FundRange works across Europe. We are especially focused on alternative lenders in consumer finance, BNPL, SME lending, leasing, and secured lending markets.
Can you help with both debt and equity? +
Yes. Our main focus is debt funding and portfolio financing, but we can also support equity introductions where relevant, especially for fintech lenders raising growth capital alongside debt funding.
What types of lenders do you work with? +
We work with non-bank lenders including consumer lenders, BNPL providers, SME lenders, leasing companies, asset finance companies, real estate-backed lenders, and other alternative credit originators.
How fast can investor introductions start? +
If your portfolio data and investor materials are ready, first investor discussions can often start within several days after the initial discussion. If your materials are not ready, we first help structure the data and improve the funding case.
Can you help if we already have a funding partner? +
Yes. Many lenders work with FundRange even if they already have funding in place. Common reasons include refinancing, reducing funding cost, adding a second funding partner, increasing facility size, finding junior capital, or improving flexibility.
What makes FundRange different from other advisors? +
FundRange is focused specifically on alternative lending and institutional capital. We understand loan portfolios, investor metrics, credit performance, and funding structures — and the specific challenges non-bank lenders face when raising capital. This focus makes the process more targeted and more efficient than a generalist approach.
